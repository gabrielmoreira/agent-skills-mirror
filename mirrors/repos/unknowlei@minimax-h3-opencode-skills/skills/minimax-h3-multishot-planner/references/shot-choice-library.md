# MiniMax H3 Multishot Choice Library

Use this library to create context-specific choices. Do not present every entry at once and do not describe this entire library as an official closed vocabulary.

## Shot Size and Composition

- Extreme long shot: environment and scale dominate.
- Long or wide shot: full space, geography, and major movement remain visible.
- Full shot: the complete body and physical action are readable.
- Medium wide shot: body language and nearby environment remain balanced.
- Medium shot: gesture, interaction, and dialogue are emphasized.
- Medium close-up: facial performance and upper-body gesture dominate.
- Close-up: emotion, object detail, or a decisive reaction dominates.
- Extreme close-up: one facial or object detail becomes the entire visual event.
- Over-the-shoulder: foreground relationship and point of attention are explicit.
- POV: the camera adopts a character's direct viewpoint.

## Camera Treatments

Build options as complete behaviors that combine movement, speed, amplitude, tracking target, and compositional purpose.

- Static locked shot
- Slow small-amplitude push in
- Fast large-amplitude push in
- Slow controlled pull out
- Pan left or right
- Truck left or right
- Tilt up or down
- Pedestal up or down
- Arc shot with counter-pan to retain the subject
- Tracking shot matching subject speed
- Handheld follow with restrained shake
- POV movement tied to character motion
- Zoom in or out without physical camera translation
- Roll clockwise or counterclockwise for intentional disorientation
- Crane-like rise or descent revealing space

Prefer mechanically clear descriptions. Distinguish a physical push from optical zoom, a pan from lateral truck movement, and an arc from a simple pan.

## Transition and Opening Treatments

Later-shot transitions:

- Direct cut
- Match cut based on shape, pose, movement, or composition
- Cross-dissolve
- Fade transition
- Wipe motivated by a foreground object
- Whip-pan transition
- Action cut during continuous movement
- Reaction cut
- Sound bridge with J-cut or L-cut

Shot 1 openings:

- Immediate cold open on active motion
- Brief stable establishing frame before motion begins
- Fade in from black
- Reveal from behind a foreground obstruction
- Sound-led opening before the main visual action
- Extreme-detail opening that expands to reveal context
- Start on a reference-like still composition, then release into motion

## Narrative Functions

Generate concrete scene-specific variants of at least five relevant functions:

- Establish location, atmosphere, and spatial relationships
- Introduce the main subject or product
- Initiate the central action or problem
- Develop or escalate the action
- Reveal new information, scale, or transformation
- Show a reaction or emotional turn
- Demonstrate a feature, cause, or result
- Reach the visual or dramatic climax
- Resolve the action and establish the final state
- Deliver a product, title, logo, or call-to-action ending

## Performance Dimensions

Build content-specific choices that combine:

- Body action and direction
- Hand or object interaction
- Gaze target and head movement
- Facial expression and emotional change
- Pace, weight, and energy
- Starting state, continuous development, and visible result

Avoid offering a single isolated verb when a short action arc can be described.

## Sound Focus

Offer relevant combinations rather than indiscriminate layers:

- Natural ambience only
- Ambience plus synchronized Foley
- Ambience, Foley, and non-verbal human sound
- Dialogue or voice-led sound with restrained ambience
- Strong diegetic event sound with no audience-only score
- Restrained audience-only score under realistic scene sound
- Music-led audiovisual design with beat-synchronized edits
- Sound bridge connecting adjacent shots

Keep dialogue and singing inside the shot timeline. Summarize ambience, Foley, and non-verbal human sound in `overall_soundscape`. Reserve `non_diegetic_music` for audience-only music.

## Timing and Edit Rhythm

- Balanced progression: similar shot durations with a clear beginning, middle, and end
- Slow opening, fast development: establish carefully, then accelerate
- Fast opening, slow resolution: hook immediately, then hold the result
- Progressive acceleration: each shot becomes shorter toward the climax
- Progressive deceleration: rapid setup settles into a longer final image
- Hero-shot emphasis: reserve the longest duration for the key reveal or product shot
- Performance emphasis: reserve the longest duration for the most complex physical action or dialogue

Do not allocate a cut merely to fill time. Every new shot should change viewpoint, information, subject state, location, time, or dramatic emphasis.
