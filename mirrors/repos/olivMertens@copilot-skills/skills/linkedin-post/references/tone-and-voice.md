# Tone & voice

Write in the USER's voice, not a generic brand voice. The goal is a post that
reads like they wrote it.

## How to capture the voice
1. **Try their material first**: profile "About", recent posts, published
   articles. If LinkedIn blocks the fetch (HTTP 999 / login wall — common),
   ask the user to paste 1–3 recent posts or article intros.
2. **Observe and mirror**, don't caricature:
   - sentence length & rhythm (punchy vs. flowing)
   - person (first-person "I built…" vs. company "we…")
   - FR/EN usage (fully bilingual? franglais? which language leads?)
   - emoji density (none / sparse / expressive) and where they sit
   - signature devices (em-dashes, one-line punchlines, rhetorical questions,
     arrows "→", section emojis)
   - how they handle credit (name teammates, tag partners) and disclaimers
3. **State your basis**: tell the user which sample(s) you matched so they can
   correct you.

## Fallback tone menu (offer if no sample is available)
- **Builder/maker** — first person, hands-on, "here's what I shipped and how",
  concrete tech names, light humility, one crisp punchline.
- **Advisor/strategic** — outcome-led, business value first, minimal jargon,
  measured confidence, a forward-looking close.
- **Community/teacher** — generous, explains the how, invites replies, credits
  others, a clear takeaway readers can reuse.

## Anti-patterns (avoid unless the user's own voice does this)
- "I'm thrilled/excited/humbled to announce…" openers.
- Empty hype adjectives ("game-changing", "revolutionary") with no proof.
- Emoji every line; hashtag stuffing; a wall of text.
- Overclaiming ("world-first", "fully autonomous") — especially for a demo.
