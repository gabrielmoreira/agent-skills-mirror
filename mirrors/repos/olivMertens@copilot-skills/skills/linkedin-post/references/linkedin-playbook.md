# LinkedIn post playbook (2025-era best practices)

Guidance for a punchy, high-reach LinkedIn post. These are conventions, not laws
— the user's stated preferences and voice always win.

## Structure
- **Hook (line 1)** — the single most important line. It must earn the "…more"
  click. 1 short sentence, concrete, no wind-up. Avoid "I'm excited to share".
  Prefer a tension, a number, or a plain claim ("A PDF in. An audited decision
  out.").
- **White space** — short paragraphs (1–2 lines), blank line between them. Walls
  of text kill reach.
- **Body** — 3–5 short beats: the problem, what you did, how it works (name the
  concrete tech), the proof/outcome. One idea per line.
- **Bullets** — a few "→" or "·" lines for scannability, not a giant list.
- **CTA (last lines)** — one clear ask (comment / try it / read the repo).
- **Length** — ~1,300–1,900 characters is a good band for substance without
  fatigue; the visible pre-"…more" portion is ~210 characters (~3 lines) on
  desktop, less on mobile — front-load meaning there.

## Media
- **Native video** outperforms links. Upload the MP4 natively; do NOT put the
  video only as a URL.
- **Captions/subtitles**: most feed views are muted → burned-in captions or an
  uploaded SRT are essential. Provide FR and EN. LinkedIn accepts one SRT per
  video at upload (viewer toggle); for "both languages available", supply two
  SRT files (the user picks per post) or a burned-in variant per language.
- **Poster/cover**: a strong first frame or a custom cover image. Make the first
  frame legible while muted (a headline + the product name).
- **Aspect ratio**: 16:9 works; square (1:1) or vertical (4:5) take more feed
  height on mobile and often lift reach — offer to re-render if the user wants.

## Links & reach
- LinkedIn's algorithm tends to **down-rank posts with outbound links in the
  body**. Common mitigations: put the link in the **first comment**, or add it
  after the main value with a "link in comments" note. If the user marks links
  as **mandatory in-body**, respect that and just note the trade-off.
- Tag people/companies only when genuinely relevant; over-tagging is penalized.

## Hashtags
- 3–5, specific over generic. Mix one broad (#AI), one domain (#InsurTech), one
  product/tech (#Azure / #AzureAIFoundry). Place at the very end.

## Tone
- First person, confident, specific. Show, don't boast. Concrete nouns
  (services, numbers, artifacts) over adjectives.
- Accessibility: add real caption files; add alt text to images where possible.

## Compliance
- If it's a demo, say so plainly ("technical demonstration, not a product").
- If data is fictitious/among demo data, state it. Include any employer
  disclaimer the user requires, verbatim.
