---
kind: guidance
title: Field claims
---

A field claim is a proposition about the subject or execution environment whose truth must hold for a skill decision to work. Defaults, thresholds, meanings, boundaries, transformations, and fallbacks can be field claims even when imperative; unstated ones are supplied from prior belief.

Descriptions, goals, cues, and selected behaviors declare intention, not subject fact or correct realization. A requester may set a rule within their authority, but settles its subject claim only when able to establish it.

Before dependent behavior or its completion closes, each required field claim is:

- established by competent authority;
- made a runtime input or authority branch before dependent action;
- removed with the dependent behavior by authorized narrowing or withdrawal; or
- left blocking that behavior, with the settling check or authority reported.

An unestablished label is useful inventory, never clearance. When they apply, read [[guide:domain-evidence]] before accepting a claim's basis or deciding how the child skill will keep it current, and [[guide:claim-provenance]] before deciding what provenance survives for later reviewers.
