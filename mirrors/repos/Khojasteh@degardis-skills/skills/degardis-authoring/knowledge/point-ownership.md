---
kind: guidance
title: Point ownership
---

A point is the smallest semantic contribution whose removal would change what a reader can decide or understand. Different wording, construct, or local framing does not make two statements different points when either can disappear without changing what the other already lets the same reader derive. Give each point one owning statement, at the width that decides its cases, where its reader makes the decision it serves. A shared idea is not a shared point: where readers apply one idea in different work, each application is its own point, owned on its reader's path in that work's terms. One owner for all of them makes each reader hold work it is not doing, which spends the attention its own work needs, whatever the page budget allows. A generalization must retain the decision logic or name an authority reachable on every path; a slogan such as "preserve safety" cannot replace the actions it does not derive.

Bound a unit by the work its reader comes to it for, neither by an idea its parts share nor by a single decision. Name the thing that reader is working on, such as one construct or one stage of the work: the unit carries what the reader must understand about that thing before deciding any part of it, and the decisions it makes about it, so an agent refining a guide's condition first learns what a guide is. Where readers reach one unit for different work, split it along that work, however much the parts have in common.

A second occurrence is support only when it contributes a distinct premise, evidence, example, consumer-specific consequence, or checked reference. Otherwise it is another owner that can drift. Before drafting a new point, choose its owner across the whole planned change rather than file by file. Compare every added or rewritten statement with the starting source and the other proposed statements; strengthen or replace the owner, narrow the statement to its distinct contribution, or reference the owner instead of keeping a same-point restatement for emphasis or locality.

Test a rewrite with a case the old text decided: the new owner must derive the same action.
