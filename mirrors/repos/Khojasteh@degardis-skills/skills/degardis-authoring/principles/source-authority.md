---
title: Source authority
---

Nothing you do to a claim may raise its authority. Preserve qualifiers, attribution, and dates when quoting, summarizing, merging, translating, or restating. A projection remains a projection; an assertion becomes fact only when its source is authoritative for that fact.

Attach provenance when taking the claim, not later. If sources disagree, preserve the disagreement and what each source said rather than collapsing them.

Common authority leaks are: dropping qualifiers in summaries; lending one source's confidence to another while merging; restating in your own voice; repeating an inference until it reads as observed; generalizing instances into a rule; and attributing your judgment to a tool or process.

Comparable work, examples, or established practice prove what those instances do, not what is correct. Multiple instances do not increase authority; instances sharing one influence are one source counted repeatedly. Use patterns to locate questions, then settle them against something that decides them: a requirement in the material, an authority over the subject, or an observed result. Unexamined practice can propagate defects as easily as conventions.

An artifact's reader has neither your process nor the versions before it, so unless the artifact exists to record that history, write it as what holds, in terms its reader can resolve. Do not define it against an earlier version, an option weighed or rejected, the conversation that shaped it, or an assumption only the process held. A superseded or rejected option establishes nothing, and wherever it appears it is marked as such.

Reasoning about what holds, such as why a rule is right, belongs in the artifact when its purpose includes reasoning. Reasoning about the change is process, not evidence to that reader: what the artifact said before, why it changed, and what was weighed and dropped. Put each only in a record or report whose purpose includes it, such as a change record for what changed and why or a decision record for the options weighed and each one's status, and never beside the changed value merely because it explains the change.

Self-adopted objectives such as safer, cheaper, smaller, cleaner, or faster have only your authority and cannot displace an authoritative requirement. If such a goal causes a check to be skipped, report the result as unverified rather than passed.

If the target form cannot preserve a claim's authority, state what would be lost instead of overstating the claim.
