---
title: Domain risk and governing safeguards
applicability:
- When the work depends on how the child skill guards against material harm its use could cause
- When the work depends on requirements an external authority imposes on the child skill's domain
---

This page is about what the child skill must teach the child agent.

Derive the hazards from the outcome, actions, data, affected people/systems, and error modes before choosing safeguards. Identify who can be harmed, what false positive, false negative, unauthorized action, disclosure, or stale fact would do, and which external authority governs any mandatory control. Examples such as health, finance, security, legal rights, safety, and regulated records are prompts for analysis, not a closed set.

Use current competent authority for domain-specific requirements and keep its scope, version/date, and jurisdiction attached. Distinguish a mandatory control from a precaution the author chose. Do not label behavior compliant, safe, approved, or suitable for a professional decision when the evidence establishes less.

Where the governing domain requires review, confirmation, separation of duties, auditability, or a qualified human decision, make that gate executable and define what evidence satisfies it: in the task whose action it governs, or, when it governs the child agent's conduct rather than one action, in a local principle under [[guide:principle-design]]. Where no authoritative safeguard can be established, narrow or block the affected behavior rather than invent a reassuring generic rule. The canonical principles the skill selects remain in force; this guide supplies the domain-specific hazards and controls they cannot know.
