---
title: Page budgets and the compiled reading path
applicability:
- When the work depends on a compiler-reported page budget
---

Take the reading path and its budgets from the effective manual and compiler report, not from source-file length. A load problem is a reasoning problem: what is this reader being asked to hold that this request will not consume?

Reduce load by removing duplication, moving content out of universal placement when its established ownership and applicability support that move, or splitting material where the work of the readers it reaches divides. If relief would change which construct owns a provision, return to [[guide:construct-ownership]]; a budget never selects the replacement. Never reduce load by weakening a needed distinction, verification, or boundary.

## Load reduction

Begin with the narrow compiler reports that establish generated page sizes, budget headroom, knowledge closures, contributor sizes, and diagnostics. Record the affected pages and their closures before editing; source-file length does not show what one routed agent receives.

Group affected pages by the knowledge they share. A shared unit is leverage only when one coherent repair lowers every affected page that carries it; being large does not make its content expendable. Repair that shared pressure before the task-specific remainder, then remeasure. Common dispositions follow from ownership and reach:

- where several statements own one point, keep the owner that lets the reader derive every existing case and remove the copies;
- where one unit carries material for work some of its readers are not doing, split it along that work;
- where only a recognizable situation needs detail, move it out of universal placement, and let construct ownership select the new owner;
- where one task actually contains independent request classes, split it only when request-visible cues, at their manifest positions, route each class to the task meant for it; and
- where every run needs the material, keep it on the page and condense only when cases decided by the old text remain decidable from the rewrite.

These dispositions are examples, not a closed set; the ownership and reach tests decide an unlisted case.

After each coherent tranche, ask the compiler again about the affected pages rather than spending preservation effort against an obsolete state. Completion requires every compiler-measured output to fit the effective compiler's budget, every needed provision still to reach its task, no knowledge to become orphaned, and the closing compiler check to pass. Preserve useful headroom when further additions are part of the work; it is not a reason to delete needed guidance.

A skill exists so that its agent does the work well, and size constrains arrangement rather than how much needed understanding ships. A budget can expose duplication, an over-broad reading path, or a boundary worth reconsidering; it is a constraint on the selected arrangement, not the selector. Where what a task needs will not fit, report the oversize page: one made to fit by teaching its agent less reads as finished and is not.

A smaller page, a shorter body, or a lower reference count is never evidence that what was removed was redundant, only that it is gone. A page that cannot be traversed completely in a realistic run is a behavior defect, not a byte statistic. Applicability that holds on nearly every observed run is evidence to recheck the boundary its conditions draw.

A remaining margin is worth reporting only where the reader is about to spend it, and what decides that is whether material will be added as a result, never what the deliverable is called. To a judgment nobody will act on, a page inside its budget is a number with no decision attached. To a plan, to a change, or to a review whose recommendations will add material, the headroom is the constraint those additions have to fit inside, and it belongs beside them rather than in a list of findings.
