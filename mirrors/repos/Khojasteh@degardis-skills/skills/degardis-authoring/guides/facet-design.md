applicability:
- When the work depends on a facet

Whether a provision is a facet at all is decided under [[guide:construct-ownership]]; what follows keeps a facet classified there to optional informational specialization, and takes how facets are selected and composed from the effective manual.

## Informational specialization

Keep only understanding whose usefulness depends on the narrower domain; domain-specific examples of a general rule do not make the rule facet-specific. A facet may add terminology, conventions, what always holds in the domain, models of its material or setting, characteristic failure modes, where its evidence is found, what changes with a version, date, or governing body, correspondences between its terms and the skill's, capabilities, limitations, and comparable domain-specific context.

Knowledge of a domain's instruments stays descriptive unless the current task already owns their use: a facet may explain what an instrument, procedure, or service does, what evidence it can expose, or what limitations matter in the domain, but it must not instruct the agent to use it merely because the facet applies.

Apply both tests to an instance already inside the candidate facet domain. **Removal:** hold its request, material, environment, and other established conditions fixed; remove only the facet and guides reachable only from it; then rehearse the same task. The result may be more generic, less fluent in the domain, or less well-informed, but the core must still complete the declared outcome correctly. **Addition:** start from that working core task and restore the facet and facet-only guides. Everything construct ownership keeps out of a facet must remain owned by the same task. If either test fails, return the workflow obligation to construct ownership and keep only the situational understanding here.

Use the sentence-level test: if a statement can be followed correctly only after determining what task or workflow stage the agent is performing, the statement must not independently create that stage from a facet.

## Domain boundary

The agent recognizes a facet from its index entry once relevant evidence is available, so make the title identify the observable boundary, not summarize the body. Add a description only where the title alone cannot tell the boundary apart from its neighbors, and give it only what settles that: it is read before the decision, so a summary of the body there is content in the index's place.

The boundary need not be knowable at routing time, only decidable before facet-specific guidance is relied on. If the necessary property is unknown, the facet is not yet established; reassess when material or environment evidence appears rather than guessing from the task. Grouping in the index neither establishes applicability nor makes grouped facets exclusive.

Request wording establishes the same boundary only when it actually establishes the relevant material or environment property. A task name or requested operation is not a substitute.

## Facet-owned detail

A guide reachable only through a facet inherits its boundary, as construct ownership says, so moving procedural content into that guide does not make it specialization. The guide may elaborate domain-specific knowledge or describe the evidence, instruments, procedures, forms, capabilities, limitations, and behaviors relevant to a stage the current task already owns. Its applicability may depend on stage, effect, or another property of the work only to decide when that understanding is useful after the task has independently established the work; applicability must not cause the stage or effect to exist.

If the guide body assumes the facet domain, every owner path that can open it must establish that domain first. If its applicability instead identifies an independent situational condition, do not infer facet status from that independence. Return the provision to construct ownership and decide whether the task owns work under that condition before placing any domain-specific understanding here.

## Facet composition

Several situational properties can hold at once, so facet overlap is composition rather than routing. Check guidance at every supported intersection. Compatible facets may accumulate. When one domain refines another, make the refinement or exception explicit from evidence available to the agent; do not rely on manifest order, category, or whichever page opened last. If domains are meant to be mutually exclusive, their boundaries must make that exclusivity decidable from material or environment evidence.

Do not split one domain into separate facets because different tasks use different parts of its specialization: domain and current task are separate axes. Conversely, do not merge independent domain properties merely because they often co-occur.

## Skill-wide reach

Every task may meet the facet, so its body may state task-specific consequences only as consequences for a decision the current task already owns.

Treat compensating task prose as evidence of a facet ownership defect. A task should not need to mention facets, suppress or override facet instructions, or otherwise defend its workflow from specialization. If removing the facet would make such task prose meaningless, rehome the procedural owner and remove the compensation that depended on the old arrangement.

Move bulky informational specialization needed only for recognizable work inside the domain to facet-owned guides whose applicability expresses when that understanding is useful. This changes load, not workflow ownership.

After arranging the facet, check the general rule left in the core, both removal and addition tests, index boundary, evidence timing, supported overlaps, domain-specific claims, facet-only guide content, compensating task prose, and generated read path.
