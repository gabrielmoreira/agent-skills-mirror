---
title: Canonical principle contract
applicability:
- When the work depends on how a skill carries a canonical principle
---

The canonical text ships in this bundle, one page for each principle [[guide:principle-selection]] lists.

These pages are canonical because their failures and decision procedures recur across subjects. Every skill that selects one carries the same title and body, so neither may rely on one skill's subject matter, local constructs, vocabulary, artifacts, roles, or procedure. Test every added or revised term, example, and instruction against materially different skill subjects: if the same decision cannot be derived without local context or renamed local concepts, keep it out of the canonical page and put it in the child skill's knowledge or a local principle.

Read the selected file before carrying it rather than rebuilding it from a one-line account of the failure it closes: that line is the unexplained rule these exist to replace, and a child skill holding only an abridged account inherits the form without the reasoning that makes an unlisted case decidable.

The generated title and body are the canonical principle. Carry both unchanged: do not paraphrase, shorten, extend, merge, specialize, or append examples. The body ends where the authored text ends: whatever the compiler adds around a generated page is not part of it and is not carried. Compare the child skill's generated principle page with the linked canonical page; a difference in either means the canonical principle was not carried.

Use the effective manual's distinction between principle source, link placement, applicability, and generated page to rebuild the child skill's own principle source. Placement and applicability belong to the child skill's work and are decided there rather than copied unexamined: a condition taken from a skill whose work meets the failure rarely can hide the principle from one whose work meets it constantly. Those decisions do not change the canonical title or body.

A principle carries its own mechanics, so what you carry is the whole page and never the decision without the procedure that executes it. A child agent told to write a bounded brief and not told what a brief contains invents the contract its units run under, and that gap is invisible from anything but the page it was cut from — which is why a principle is never split across a page and a companion file the next author has no reason to look for.

Additional material remains separate from the canonical page. Use [[guide:construct-ownership]] to decide whether it is a local principle or knowledge and where it belongs. In an existing source, treat a local principle as its own contribution while reconciling it; it cannot stand in for, or be merged into, a canonical one because its execution effect appears equivalent.
