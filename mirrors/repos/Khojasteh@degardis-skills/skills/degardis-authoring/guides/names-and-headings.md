---
title: Names and headings
applicability:
- When the work depends on how a source names something
---

Take each construct's rendering from the effective manual, then judge a name or heading by the decision it serves where its reader meets it, rather than by how it looks in the source.

## Link titles

A title the reader uses to decide whether to open a separate page must distinguish that page without standing in for its reasoning. A sentence invites the reader to act on the title and never open the file where the reasoning lives. Keep these titles to a noun phrase, in the fewest words that tell one apart from its neighbors.

## Headings

A heading is a label, not content. It heads text the reader is already reading whole, such as a knowledge unit on a task page or a section of a body, so it has three jobs: mark where that text starts, tell it apart from its neighbors, and give readers and authors a name to find it by. A noun phrase naming its subject, in the fewest words that do those jobs, does all three; a knowledge unit's kind is already the heading the compiler places above it. A sentence, an imperative, or a summary of the text below is content in a heading's place: the reader can act on it without the qualifications only the body carries, it becomes a second owner of the body's point, and it costs its bytes on every page that carries it.

A task title is both a heading and the router's label for a class of requested work, so it may name that work as a request would; like any heading, it neither instructs nor summarizes.

## Identifiers

Every reference targets the construct's id, so name what the construct is, never where it is placed, who owns it, or when it applies: those change without the construct changing, and each change would otherwise rename it and every reference to it. A principle or guide id is also read far from the page it names, wherever the bundle lists it, so make it distinctive: a compound naming the construct's own subject, never a single common word that could name many things.
