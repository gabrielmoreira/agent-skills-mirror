Whether a provision is a principle is decided under [[guide:construct-ownership]]; once it is one, it holds on every path that names it: take from the effective manual which runs read a principle the manifest names and which read one a task names. Applicability confines it within those paths to the situations where its failure can occur. A canonical principle's title and body are fixed, while its placement and applicability, like everything about a local principle, are the skill's own to decide.

## Local principle content

Write a local principle to the standard of the canonical pages. A name alone decides nothing, so its page states the failure it prevents, carries the reasoning that decides an unlisted case, and keeps its whole procedure on its own page: what it weighs, and the checks, deferrals, and stops that follow. It may assume its field and use the field's vocabulary, and it opens with that failure rather than with when to consult it, which its reader has just read on the link.

## Placement and applicability

Placement and applicability are separate decisions, and confusing them puts guidance where its reader will not meet it. Take the mechanics of both from the effective manual, including that prose around an inline reference to a principle creates no condition the compiler carries, so each condition is stated once, in applicability.

Test the result against the failure the principle closes: every task whose work can meet it must reach the guidance, and no unrelated task should pay to load it. If one task needs a materially different condition, that is a second principle rather than an override.

Judge a gate by reach rather than frequency. Guidance needed by every reachable run is unconditional; guidance needed only in a recognizable situation names that situation, however common or rare it is. A rare condition does not strand material when the reader can recognize it before opening the page, and a common condition is not universal merely because it often holds.

## Condition content

Write the condition from the failure the principle closes: the observable moment in the work at which that failure becomes possible, before the decision the standard governs. Because one condition decides reach on every path that names the principle, it names no task, route, or activity standing for one; a path whose runs need the principle while its condition does not hold shows a condition that names its readers rather than the failure, so rewrite it at the width of the failure instead of appending an item. Make every item decidable before the principle is read, from what any naming path can observe.

Some canonical principles fail when gated late: let bounded delegation's applicability hold before the need and authority are decided rather than only before an instance is launched, completion evidence's hold from the start of the work toward the outcome, since its final conditions are due before any activity counts as progress, and not only before the completion claim, and sensitive material's reach the moment the material is opened and not only the moment it leaves.

## Items

One holding item is enough for the principle to apply, and conditions that must all hold share one item, so each item is one situation that alone puts the failure within reach, and the list holds each situation once. Decide what is one situation by what the reader checks, not by how the prose divides.

Situations recognized from one observable fact are one: name that fact once, at the width of the failure, rather than listing its instances as sibling items or as a series joined by *or* inside one, since the next instance would leave the principle unread. Situations recognized from different evidence or at different moments are separate even where one sentence could join them: joined in one item, they hide that either suffices alone, make the reader parse which clause holds, and let an edit meant for one change the other. Material entering the run and material leaving it, for example, are checked at different moments against different facts, so each takes its own item.

Test each item alone: suppose it is the only one that holds, and ask whether the failure is then within reach. An item that fails is part of another situation, such as a precondition or qualifier, rather than one of its own: join it with *and* to the item it qualifies, or delete it where it qualifies none; standing alone, it loads the principle wherever it holds. An item that needs a sibling's words to be read, through *also*, *otherwise*, or a pronoun pointing across, depends on that sibling the same way, so each item stands alone, with its own opener.

Before keeping two items, find for each a case that satisfies it and not the other. Where neither has one, they are one situation worded twice, whether as paraphrases or as a series of synonyms, and the same holds for two clauses of one item: keep the wording that decides it most directly and delete the rest, since copies left in place drift apart until they name different situations. Where only one has such a case, it is the wider and the other adds nothing: delete the narrower, unless the wider item is broader than the need, which makes the wider the one to narrow. The number of items follows from these tests and is never a target.

A long list that survives these tests is the usual sign that the principle closes more than one failure. Ask which failure each item's situation puts within reach: where items reach different failures, split a local principle into one principle per failure, each with its own condition. A canonical principle is carried whole, so a long list on one points back at the list: check its items for instances of one situation.
