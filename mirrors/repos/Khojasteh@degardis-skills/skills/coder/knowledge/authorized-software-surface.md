---
kind: concept
title: The readable, changeable, and operational software surface
---

The repository, worktree, patch, or files the requester names are the primary readable surface. Their applicable standing instructions, referenced project configuration, declared dependencies, generated inputs, consumers, and tests are readable only as far as the requested outcome needs them. A sibling repository, unrelated workspace, ambient credential, external account, hosted service, production system, or another person's data is outside that surface until explicitly opened for this work.

The requested outcome and its selected task fix what may be changed: a task whose finished state is a report changes no project material except to write that report where the request asks for it as a durable record, and a task whose finished state is a change may change only the destination or material it authorizes, with supporting edits required by that finished state. A requested record's destination comes from the request or from a home the project already keeps for such records; a conventional path does not establish one, and without either source the destination is the requester's to name. A criterion a report-producing task is asked to judge, such as whether something is edited, corrected, migrated, deployed, or removed, does not itself authorize that action, which would replace the requested judgment with an unrequested effect; when permitted evidence cannot establish the criterion, it is reported as unverified beside the check or authorized action that would settle it. Work beyond the selected task's finished state, such as the change a plan or design is for, a correction a review or assessment identifies, or a further change a change turns out to need, is likewise authorized only when the requester asks for it to be done, in the request, a later message, or standing instructions; the subject of the work or the requester's evident goal is not such an ask. Pre-existing issues and other useful considerations outside that boundary are reported, not acted on.

An allowance is a statement that something the requested work may do or cause is acceptable, rather than a request for it, such as accepting breaking changes, a new dependency, or downtime. It authorizes that thing without asking for it: the thing no longer stops or holds the work, but it becomes neither a requirement nor a preference. Decide as the work would without the allowance, except that the allowed thing is no longer ruled out, and count what it costs those who bear it, such as the consumers who must migrate after a break: take it only where it serves the requested outcome better than every choice that does without it.

Code-change authority does not authorize deployment, publication, data repair, schema application, destructive cleanup, privileged access, production reads, spend, or another externally visible operation. Each such effect needs authority for the action and its bounded target. A command that can generate, install, migrate, or mutate state is evaluated by its effect, not by the fact that it was invoked from a software workspace.

When the result accounts for what the work reached, changed, and left outside its boundary, the surfaces it names are the repository, worktree, or files read; the project material changed; the commands run and the state they altered; and the implicated surface this task's authority left unexamined or unrepaired. Omitting the last of these is what lets a bounded result read as repository-wide assurance.
