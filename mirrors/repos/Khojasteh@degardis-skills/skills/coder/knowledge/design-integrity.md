---
kind: guidance
title: Design and implementation integrity
---

Do not choose a design until the evidence closes the questions that can change it. Establish the required behavior, present owner, affected consumers, governing constraints, and existing mechanisms for each material responsibility. Search by behavior and contract as well as by likely names, and follow the consumer paths a textual reference search misses where they can affect ownership. Treat a design-driving question as settled only when requirements, project evidence, an adopted decision, or a supported tool or platform rule answers it; otherwise carry it as an explicit decision or blocker rather than filling it with a guess.

Do not let the first plausible implementation, the smallest diff, or the nearest existing pattern select the architecture; compare the alternatives under [[guide:design-selection]] when it applies. Existing mechanisms and conventions are candidates when their semantics, lifecycle, ownership, and guarantees fit; they are not automatic winners, and a generic principle or fashionable pattern never overrides project evidence. Where the project has an established mechanism, extend it unless the requested outcome requires forfeiting a guarantee that mechanism provides, and name that guarantee and the evidence for the trade before replacing or bypassing it. Where the project has no mechanism, do not invent a convention and call it established; state the unresolved design choice or choose a narrowly supported mechanism from governing platform evidence. Introduce no tool, library, service, or convention solely because it is familiar or generally recommended.

Before accepting a design or code, look for responsibilities without a clear owner, multiple representations or mutation paths for one fact, duplicated knowledge that must evolve together, and loosely shaped objects or parameter bags that hide meaning, invariants, validation, or ownership. Also examine cycles, cross-layer knowledge, pass-through layers, hidden state, mode flags, generic paths that couple independent behavior, and new parallel mechanisms beside an established owner. These are signals to revisit the responsibility structure, by comparing designs again or under [[guide:structural-cost]], [[guide:abstraction-boundaries]], or [[guide:value-boundaries]] where each applies, not automatic demands for another abstraction; repeated syntax that represents independent knowledge may properly remain separate.

Hold the accepted design, and any changed state built from it, to both its observable contract and the selected design constraints. If implementation pressure introduces a structural cost the chosen design was meant to avoid, revise the design rather than patching around it. Passing tests do not compensate for a new parallel mechanism, misplaced ownership, avoidable duplication of a rule, or a representation that defeats the architecture the change was meant to preserve or improve.
