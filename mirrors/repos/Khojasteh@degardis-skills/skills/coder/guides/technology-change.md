---
title: Technology change
applicability:
- When the version or identity of a technology the project has adopted changes
---

Establish separate identities for what was requested, declared, resolved, installed, and actually executed; disagreement among them is evidence, not a detail to smooth over. Establish the destination version, support horizon, dependent ecosystem, and any choice among its incompatible component, module, concurrency, routing, hosting, format, edition, or dialect models before mapping work. Prefer an existing destination-side model that project evidence already selects; otherwise decide from the responsibilities and project conventions, asking only when viable choices materially change support, security, or the work required. Do not quietly select an older, wider, or prerelease target to avoid work.

Classify the move from the boundary it crosses, not the request's vocabulary, version increment, or whether the identity keeps its name. Determine whether the identity changes, responsibilities are re-expressed through a different model, and an authoritative in-place update path covers this project's peers, plugins, and extensions. Prefer a supported version-aware update, codemod, or project-native migration tool, inspect everything it changes, and finish what it reports as partial. Classify uncertain evidence as the deeper move until the destination authority settles it, record the finding behind the classification in re-readable working state, and revisit it when later evidence contradicts that finding. A compatibility mechanism that still builds is not the completed move.

Enumerate the footprint by identity and ownership across the whole authorized tree: package coordinates, module paths, imports, namespaces, extensions, configuration keys, images, jobs, declarations, constraints, catalogs or bills of materials, locks, installed and vendored state, generators, toolchains, editor and analysis settings, build and deployment configuration, infrastructure, workers, automation, code, tests, schemas, data formats, telemetry, runbooks, documentation, and external consumers. Classify every hit as a responsibility, incidental reference, or unresolved; record every direct, transitive, runtime, build, plugin, peer, and optional relationship in re-readable working state, and reconcile each version at the declaration that owns it rather than the first manifest or consumer pin found. When judging a delivered move, establish the prior footprint from the plan, base revision, or diff before searching the final tree for survivors.

Read only the relevant parts of the actual destination version's authoritative migration, compatibility, release, and advisory material. Turn each stated requirement into a question about this project and retain its source, applicable answer, contradiction, or unavailable fact. Treat remembered behavior as a checklist, neighboring-version guidance as inapplicable evidence, and unread or contradictory requirements as unresolved risk or blockers rather than favorable assumptions. Resolver metadata establishes selection, not behavior.

Map each material responsibility and guarantee of the current side to a named capability of the actual destination version at the same layer. Mark each mapping as covered, covered by an additional component needing approval, approved to change behavior, or unresolved. Verify applicable ordering, stability, equality, hashing, locale, encoding, precision, overflow, complexity, allocation, failure, cancellation, security, concurrency, and compatibility semantics against the preservation contract. Express the result through the destination's own established model; do not port a utility it supplies, translate syntax or abstractions line by line, or recreate the abandoned model behind wrappers. Whether the project's own code written against the current side's model is re-expressed in the destination's model or only adapted to it follows [[guide:implementation-modernization]]. Record why custom code remains in re-readable working state. An unresolved critical responsibility blocks implementation.

Distinguish direct declarations from transitive selections. Use a targeted resolver explanation to identify the direct constraint that owns a transitive conflict, advisory, or incompatibility. Prefer updating that owner; use a supported constraint or override only when necessary, and take direct ownership of a transitive version only as a last resort. Every override or promoted declaration records scope, reason, owner, lifetime, and removal condition. Do not silence peer, engine, or platform warnings or use force, ignore, legacy, unsafe, or bypass options to conceal incompatibility.

Before source migration, update only the authorized owning declarations and regenerate resolved state with the project's adopted, preferably scoped, operation under [[guide:dependency-and-artifact-integrity]]. Account for development servers, watchers, mounted containers, language servers, and other consumers before mutating an installed tree; prefer a declaration-only operation when it cannot be confirmed idle. Prove the intended selection, preserve registry, integrity, lock, and reproducibility policies, and explain every addition, removal, upgrade, downgrade, or transitive movement outside the target before relying on the result. Remove a declaration only after its last consumer is gone.

Record the behavior to preserve, how binding it is, and independent acceptance evidence in re-readable working state before changing declarations. Derive preservation strength from supported consumers, extension points, obligations, persisted formats, accepted criteria, and operational procedures rather than from the cost of preserving it. Where exact preservation needs an unapproved mechanism or a binding behavior must change, stop that part for the owning decision before proceeding.

Staged coexistence of the two sides follows [[guide:temporary-coexistence]].

After the move, search for the retired identity and dynamic consumers, and remove the abandoned side except remainders the coexistence contract still justifies. Confirm the real destination path executes rather than a fallback. Completion states what runs now, the destination actually selected and executed, preservation evidence, resolver movement, authority-transfer status, unresolved compatibility work, and anything not retired.
