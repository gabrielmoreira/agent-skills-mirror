---
title: Comparing performance evidence
applicability:
- When a claim rests on comparing performance measurements
- When a performance check is to be kept in the project
---

Before measuring, fix the primary metric, workload and data shape, environment and configuration, warmup policy, sampling unit, repetitions, distribution or summary, outlier or exclusion rule, stopping rule, uncertainty treatment, and minimum worthwhile effect. Name which variables must remain equivalent and which may legitimately differ. Reuse a recent result only when its revision, build mode, runtime, resource limits, configuration, data, environment, and protocol match the question; otherwise measure the narrowest missing baseline and separate pre-existing functional, environmental, and performance failures.

Measure elapsed time with a monotonic clock whose resolution suits the interval, and prevent dead-code elimination, constant folding, deferred evaluation, or an unused result from removing the work. Where resource contention, caches, compilation, startup, background work, network and storage state, thermal or power behavior, or measurement overhead can affect the metric, control that drift or record it in re-readable working state. Preserve independent samples at the level where variation occurs; repeated observations inside one process, host, or shared state are not automatically independent. Interleave or randomize baseline and candidate runs where order, drift, caching, or shared infrastructure can bias them.

Keep attribution and comparison distinct: a profile locates work, while only a comparable measurement establishes that changing it mattered. Report practical effect size with dispersion or an interval in the outcome's units. Do not report the fastest run, let an average hide the relevant tail, treat statistical significance as practical value, or read a small sample's inconclusive result as equivalence.

For throughput, capacity, or tail latency, define offered load, open- or closed-loop arrival behavior, operation and payload mix, pacing, ramp, concurrency, duration, queue behavior, and failure accounting. Before generating load, establish target authority, data approval, environment isolation or explicit live-system permission, bounds on duration, concurrency, volume, storage and spend, cleanup, and an observable stop condition. Establish that the generator and observer can sustain the intended rate without their CPU, connections, descriptors, ports, DNS, queues, timeouts, or network becoming the bottleneck. Compare offered and achieved load and account for coordinated omission when response waits suppress scheduled requests. Locate the first point where throughput stops rising or latency or errors leave budget, together with the first saturated resource and its queueing, backpressure, or collapse behavior. Do not run interfering measurements concurrently.

To localize a regression, first establish a cheaper and a more expensive reference point across revision, configuration, data volume, dependency, host, or deployment, and stop the range search if variation can explain the delta. Narrow the cheapest plausible axis before bisecting code, measure historical revisions in isolated disposable state rather than rewriting the active workspace, and connect the narrowed change to the measured resource; temporal correlation alone is inconclusive. Distinguish cost added by a change from cost exposed when an index, cache, buffer, pagination, saturation, or configuration threshold was crossed.

Retain a benchmark, budget, or gate only when its workload is representative, its variation is smaller than the regression it must detect, someone owns its baseline and failure response, and it has a stable place to run. Derive tolerances from the contract and observed variation rather than a round percentage. Prefer a count, size, query-count, or allocation assertion over wall-clock timing when it detects the same regression, and keep timing thresholds out of ordinary unit runs unless calibrated infrastructure already supports them. Adding a benchmark or profiler dependency or a continuous-integration gate is a separately authorized project change; do not retain flaky timing checks, retry-until-green logic, or throwaway experiments.

Credit an effect only when the observed difference exceeds the declared uncertainty and minimum worthwhile effect under comparable conditions; report the comparison as inconclusive when the method cannot distinguish the claimed effect.
