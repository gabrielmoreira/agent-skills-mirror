---
title: Performance cost domains
applicability:
- When a performance cost must be located rather than only measured
---

Attribute material cost before choosing a remedy. Common, non-exhaustive domains are:

- **Algorithmic growth:** rule it in from a measured growth curve across meaningful input sizes, not asymptotic notation or one timing. Weigh constants, locality, preprocessing, and the scale that actually occurs, and verify outputs independently; a more complex structure that pays off only beyond the representative scale is not an improvement.
- **Memory and allocation:** identify whether the binding cost is temporary allocation, retained or peak footprint, copying, fragmentation, collection pressure, native or mapped memory, or a leak. Establish what the instrument covers and pair heap evidence with a process-level measure when cost may sit outside the managed heap. Observe repeated or concurrent work, and verify ownership, lifetime, cleanup, and bounds for pooling, reuse, caching, or manual lifetime management. Do not trade unbounded retention, a shared mutable buffer, or stale pooled state for a narrow allocation reduction; allocation count alone does not establish latency.
- **Storage, database, network, and other I/O:** count calls, queries, round trips, and transferred bytes, looking first for one access per item or iteration. Validate plans, index use, and cardinality with representative volumes and parameter distributions. When batching, streaming, or parallelizing, preserve transaction scope, pool limits, pagination, retries, timeouts, and partial-failure behavior; include the write cost of an index or materialization. Do not hide unchanged round trips behind concurrency or a cache, or over-read to make one call look cheaper.
- **Concurrency and saturation:** measure throughput, tail latency, errors, and resource use across offered load. Establish ordering, cancellation, backpressure, fairness, retry, duplicate-work, and cleanup semantics; bound workers, queues, connections, and memory, and identify the first saturated resource. Higher average throughput does not justify violated tail, reliability, or ordering guarantees, unsafe sharing, arbitrary sleeps, unbounded queues, or unobserved fire-and-forget failures.
- **Caching:** require measured repeated identical work and prefer removing that work outright when possible. Define invalidation for every state transition that can create staleness; verify key scope for identity, tenant, authorization, locale, and version; and measure hit rate, miss cost, memory growth, eviction, concurrent-miss stampedes, cold start, and the fallback when the cache is unavailable. A cache is not a default remedy and may not silently serve stale, cross-tenant, or unauthorized data.
- **Startup and artifact size:** separate cold and warm start, initialization, loading, compilation, artifact transfer, parsing, and first use, then measure the phase the outcome names on a representative target. Distinguish transferred, decompressed, parsed, and resident size. Deferred work is not a gain when it lengthens total wait, moves failure to a worse moment, extends resource lifetime, or makes the first interaction fetch what the reported artifact omitted.
- **Energy, provisioned capacity, and billed cost:** connect measured resource consumption and representative utilization to the provider's current published pricing or platform terms rather than recalled prices. Account for idle behavior, autoscaling and minimums, storage, transfer, retries, and operational overhead; state period, workload, environment, pricing assumptions, cost per unit, and total. Confirm that a resource reduction reaches the billed dimension and was not shifted to another service, resource, or device.

Use a profile, trace, plan, counter, telemetry series, or scaling experiment capable of separating the plausible domains. Enter only domains that own a material share, and apply the same frame to an evidenced domain not listed here: name its mechanism, constraint, and measurement rather than forcing it into the nearest category. An optimization in one domain can move cost or correctness risk into another, so measure the affected guardrails as well as the target. Correctness does not become negotiable because a performance effect is large.
