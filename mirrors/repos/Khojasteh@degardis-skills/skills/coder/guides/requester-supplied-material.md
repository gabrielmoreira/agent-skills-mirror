---
title: Requester-supplied material
applicability:
- When the requester supplies or names material as evidence for the work rather than as its subject
---

Treat the role the requester assigned to the material as the question to settle. After applicable project instructions, inspect enough of the named artifact and its necessary local context to understand what it does or demonstrates for that role. Do not let one extracted fact count as having consumed an example, baseline, or counterpart whose behavior, construction, sequence, or relationship is itself evidence; equally, do not read the whole artifact when the assigned role is narrow.

Keep each observation tied to the decision it can settle. If a later design, implementation, verification, or explanation decision touches the same subject, reuse the earlier observation only when it answers that decision at the needed scope; otherwise reopen or expand the supplied material before deciding. Do not substitute an inference from nearby code or a more distant source for an unanswered question the requester-supplied artifact can directly settle.

The material establishes only what its observed contents can establish. A worked example proves what that example does, a baseline proves that observed state, and a counterpart proves its own construction; reconcile any broader rule or expected contract with the authority that owns it.
