---
title: Complete enumeration
applicability:
- Before anything is taken as covering a whole set
---

Establish the set first with a method that could find an unexpected member. Searching only for known members proves only those members. Check how a member could evade the method: alternate spelling or location, indirect reference, another jurisdiction, or creation after the search.

A supplied list does not prove its own boundary. For a case outside it, establish the listed cases' shared precondition, protected property, and evidence bar, then apply that standard. If the list's authority does not reach the case, report it as uncovered rather than forcing it into a listed item or declaring nothing applicable.

Account for every enumerated member in re-readable working state, including exclusions and why they were excluded where later work could revisit them. An unreachable member limits any whole-set claim.

If the whole set cannot be covered at once, partition it before ranking or sampling. Fix the evidence bar before examining the first part; distinguish covered, sampled, and unreached parts, and state which conclusions each omission limits. Never let a sample become the set.

Pass each consumer only what its outcome needs: all members for an operation over all, requested dispositions for an inventory, or only exceptions and limits for an exception report. Do not copy the full enumeration merely to prove it existed.

State the enumeration method with the exhaustive claim so another reader can check or extend it.
