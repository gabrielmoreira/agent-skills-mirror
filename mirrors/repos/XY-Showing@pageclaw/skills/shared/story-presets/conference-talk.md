# Conference Talk Preset

## Applies When

Use when organizing content for a talk or another narrative experienced in sequence.

## Showing Focus

Help the speaker show an argument or exploration as a sequence the audience can follow. An opening may establish what matters, the progression should make relationships among ideas clear, and a conclusion may bring the material together when appropriate.

## Flexible scaffold

Draw as useful from the audience's context or starting point, an opening tension, an argument or exploration arc, evidence, transitions, a conclusion, and optional speaker guidance. A scene is a narrative unit, not a slide; any part may be omitted, merged, or reordered.

## Important distinctions

Distinguish evidence, inference, questions, and proposals. Make transitions carry genuine logical progression. Speaker guidance may add delivery cues but no new facts.

## Editorial lens

Consider what the audience should be able to see and follow at each stage, and whether transitions make the relationships among ideas clear.

## Anti-patterns

Do not reverse-engineer the story from a fixed slide count or layout. Do not disguise an agenda, slogans, or stacked data as narrative, and do not invent cases or evidence for completeness.
