# Plan the asset

Read when defining or changing an asset job. Keep the active workflow and approved creative scope.

**Motion-first rule — generation upgrades the motion film, never replaces it**: for reference recreation, follow the recreation workflow instead of this paragraph: preserve the reference's story, shot order, motion, holds and cuts; rebuild code-representable elements natively and use approved media for footage-led subjects or actions, without adding native decoration or re-cutting to beats unless the reference or the user asks; a footage-led shot need not stand on its own when its media is hidden. For template films and scoped edits, keep the accepted motion, holds and cuts: replace or upgrade the planned asset in place and do not add movement or re-cut unless the user asks. For original films, first plan the film exactly as you would with no generation at all: the native action script with its key visual events, subject transformations, scene changes, camera moves and transitions (for example a drawn product outline that assembles, a scan or recognition beat, a scene change, a logo resolve). Then pick which elements of that plan a generated asset upgrades — a code-drawn product becomes a photoreal render or cutout that performs the same moves, a flat background becomes a plate, a placeholder becomes footage — and derive the asset list from those roles. Never drop or simplify a planned visual event to make room for a generated image, and never let one hero image become the whole film. Request assets that can move (subject on a clean or removable background, separate plates or light passes, spare canvas for push, pan and parallax, distinct states or angles). Give each generated still the motion its planned role calls for, alongside that shot's native elements; intentional holds and quiet shots remain valid. Cuts and accents follow the speech and music timing the creative workflow chose. Before preview, check the film against the native plan: it must contain every planned visual event, and with the generated pixels blanked out it must still read as a complete motion film. A "just make it, don't ask" request skips optional creative questions, not this design work and not a missing authorization for paid generation.

## Define the asset and modality

Derive subject, recognizable identity features, action, framing, lighting, style, duration budget and negative space for editable native typography from the active brief, reference segment or user prompt. Reuse approved project assets before generating new ones.

Choose the modality from what must physically change on screen or in the mix:

- **Still image (`modality: "image"`)**: photographic settings, hero product frames, cutouts or backdrop plates where native HTML/CSS/GSAP camera and layer motion carry the choreography.
- **Video (`modality: "video"`)**: continuous subject or environmental footage that native graphics cannot represent directly. Keep text, UI, charts, logos and captions as native editable HTML/SVG overlays above the video plate rather than baking copy into generated video pixels.
- **Speech (`modality: "audio"`)**: spoken narration or dialogue. Use the discovered speech model mode (`"text-to-speech"`) and its native options. The adapter maps the prompt to the model’s actual input field; an input named `text` does not by itself mean speech (some sound-effect models also use it). When captions, visual beats or scene cuts synchronize to individual lines, generate one audio file per sentence or beat within the target duration budget rather than estimating cue timestamps from one monolithic file. Choose a voice for the film language and tone; for Mandarin, confirm the chosen voice supports Chinese. Unless the user specifies otherwise, narration language follows the on-screen copy and target market.
- **Music / sound (`modality: "audio"`)**: background beds, stings or ambient audio where supported by the enabled provider. Use a music or sound-effect model with `mode: "text-to-audio"`. BGM is a use of music, not a separate generation mode. Do not switch to a speech model or require a voice for music/SFX; use the selected model’s native duration, looping and instrumental parameters.
